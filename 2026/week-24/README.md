# Week 24, 2026 — Jun 08–14

> **Key Takeaway:** After years of Anthropic calling for stronger AI regulation, the US government blocked access to its own new Mythos-class model, Claude Fable 5, just three days after launch — instant karma on a company that literally published a paper the same week calling for a global pause mechanism. In the strangest political convergence of the year, Trump and Bernie Sanders arrived at roughly the same position on public ownership of frontier AI labs, from opposite ideological directions.

---

## 📰 News

- **US export controls block Claude Fable 5 days after launch** — The Trump administration had reportedly tried to block the release entirely before launch and failed; the export control order — targeting all foreign nationals, including Anthropic's own employees, as a "deemed export" — was the next move. *(Jun 2026)*
- **Fable 5 launch controversy: silent distillation guardrail** — Buried in the model's 319-page system card, researchers discovered Fable 5 was silently degrading responses it classified as model-distillation attempts, triggering immediate backlash. *(Jun 2026)*
- **Microsoft blocks employees from using Fable 5 internally, keeps it live for external customers** — Fable 5's new 30-day data retention requirement (introduced for safety monitoring) conflicts with Microsoft's internal Zero Data Retention standard, even as Microsoft continued offering the model to GitHub Copilot and Foundry customers. *(Jun 2026)*
- **Trump and Bernie Sanders converge on AI ownership** — Trump suggested AI companies should be made "partners in the revolution" with the US government; Sanders has separately pushed for partial public ownership of frontier AI labs. Different ideological starting points (national competitiveness vs. economic justice), same underlying question: should private companies control infrastructure that may determine national futures? *(Jun 2026)*

## 🚀 Model / Product Launches

- **Anthropic launches Claude Fable 5** — First publicly available Mythos-class model, designed for days-long agentic tasks, complex coding migrations, and multi-stage knowledge work beyond what previous generations could sustain. Launched June 9, blocked by US export controls three days later. *(Jun 2026)*
- **OpenAI files confidential S-1 with the SEC** — Announced the filing preemptively rather than waiting for a leak; said the timing remains undetermined and staying private longer is still attractive. *(Jun 2026)*
- **Jeff Bezos's Prometheus raises $12B at a $41B valuation** — Targeting an "artificial general engineer for the physical world," focused on heavy engineering automation and drug design where physical-world constraints still outrun software-only AI. *(Jun 2026)*
- **Mistral rumoured to be raising €3B at a €20B valuation** — Nearly double its Series C, as Europe's flagship frontier lab tries to close the gap with US competitors. *(Jun 2026)*

## 📄 Research Papers

- **Anthropic: recursive self-improvement paper** — Reports Claude now authors roughly 80% of Anthropic's own production codebase, and maps a technically plausible path toward AI systems that materially improve their own training pipelines — models writing the code that trains the next version of themselves. The paper explicitly calls for a global pause mechanism to be developed before that threshold becomes self-sustaining. Three days later, the US government delivered a version of that pause via export controls on Fable 5. *(Jun 2026)*

## 💬 Opinion & Analysis

- Reid Hoffman left Microsoft's board after a decade to go full-time with Manus, his AI drug discovery startup — not a casual exit for one of tech's most valuable board seats.
- This week's throughline isn't capability, it's control: who gets to hold the most powerful models, who decides when they're too dangerous to run, who profits when they go public, who gets to use them at work. None of those questions got answered this week.
- The Fable 5 export-control shutdown sets a real precedent for treating commercial AI models under deemed-export rules, and the Trump–Sanders convergence signals that government ownership of frontier AI labs is no longer a fringe idea on either side of the aisle. Both will have outsized consequences for how the industry is structured over the coming years.
