# Week 29, 2026 — Jul 06–12

> **Key Takeaway:** Meta shipped and killed an AI image feature in three days flat after it defaulted to letting users generate images of friends by tagging their Instagram accounts — a self-inflicted wound that needed no external feedback to see coming. Meanwhile OpenAI had its busiest week of the year (GPT-5.6 GA, a new agentic ChatGPT Work product, and killing its own nine-month-old Atlas browser), and OpenAI's fifth senior safety leader in two years headed for the exit.

---

## 📰 News

- **Meta ships and pulls "Muse Image" in three days** — First image model out of Alexandr Wang's Superintelligence Labs let users generate images of friends and creators by tagging their public Instagram accounts, enabled by default with opt-out buried in settings. Gone by Friday evening after backlash; Meta said the feature "missed the mark." *(Jul 2026)*
- **OpenAI's head of safety Johannes Heidecke departs** — Leaving after a reorg folds safety teams into research under Mia Glaese; at least the fifth senior OpenAI safety leader to exit in two years — a pattern now more informative than any single departure. *(Jul 2026)*
- **DeepSeek reportedly designing its own inference chip, fabbed at SMIC** — Rather than relying on TSMC, as ~70% of AI compute demand shifts to inference — precisely where Chinese hardware is trying to build leverage and reduce exposure to US chips. *(Jul 2026)*

---

## 🚀 Model / Product Launches

- **OpenAI ships GPT-5.6 to general availability in three tiers** — Pitched on intelligence-per-dollar rather than raw capability alone; Sol scores 53.6 on the Agents' Last Exam, 13.1 points ahead of Claude Fable 5, with Terra and Luna claimed to beat Fable at roughly a sixteenth of the cost. OpenAI now explicitly benchmarks on accuracy and cost, not accuracy alone. *(Jul 2026)*
- **OpenAI launches ChatGPT Work** — An agent that reaches into Slack, Notion, Drive, and Microsoft 365 to produce finished decks, sheets, and docs, capable of grinding on a project for hours. *(Jul 2026)*
- **OpenAI kills the ChatGPT Atlas browser** — Nine months after launch, folding agentic browsing into a desktop super-app instead. *(Jul 2026)*
- **Claude Cowork expands to web and mobile** — Previously a desktop-only app launched in January; users can now start a task at their desk, get pinged on their phone, and collect output later from a laptop. *(Jul 2026)*
- **Lovable in talks to raise $300M at $13.2B; ElevenLabs discussing tender offer at ~$22B** — Lovable's valuation roughly doubles its December mark on $500M annualised revenue and 146 employees; ElevenLabs' figure doubles its February valuation. Both companies have real revenue behind the numbers. *(Jul 2026)*

## 💬 Opinion & Analysis

- Meta didn't need user feedback to know the Instagram-tagging image feature was a bad idea — they shipped it anyway, because the cost of asking permission first is a slower launch and the cost of not asking is an apology. It's hard not to worry when a company this powerful ships something this reckless, with this much potential to hurt people.
- The pace of product releases shows no sign of slowing: GPT-5.6, ChatGPT Work, Cowork going mobile, and a Chinese inference chip in design — all inside a single week.
- OpenAI's fifth safety-leadership departure in two years is a pattern worth tracking on its own, independent of any single individual's stated reasons for leaving.
