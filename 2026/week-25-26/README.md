# Weeks 25–26, 2026 — Jun 15–28

> **Key Takeaway:** The question of the fortnight was who actually gets to press the launch button — and increasingly the answer involves governments on both sides of the Pacific. The US forced Anthropic to pull Mythos 5 and Fable 5 entirely under export-control authority (reportedly triggered by Amazon's CEO flagging Fable 5's cyberattack-relevant outputs to officials), then partially relented; OpenAI's GPT-5.6 preview was restricted to government-vetted partners at the administration's request; and China ordered Meta to unwind its $2B Manus acquisition on national-security grounds.

---

## 📰 News

- **Anthropic forced to pull Mythos 5 and Fable 5 under US export controls** — Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick invoked export-control authority, treating foreign nationals (including Anthropic's own employees) as a "deemed export." The reported trigger: Amazon CEO Andy Jassy told government officials that Amazon researchers had used Fable 5 to surface information usable in cyberattacks. Days later, the government partially relented — Mythos 5 can now go to a vetted list of 100+ critical-infrastructure operators and Fortune 500 firms without a license, though Fable, the consumer-facing model, stays restricted. *(Jun 2026)*
- **OpenAI's GPT-5.6 preview limited to government-vetted "trusted partners"** — Restricted at the administration's request; OpenAI complied while explicitly calling the arrangement a short-term step it doesn't want to see become permanent. *(Jun 2026)*
- **China orders Meta to unwind its $2B Manus acquisition** — Meta began cutting the Chinese-founded startup off from internal systems after Beijing ordered the deal reversed on national-security grounds; Manus's founders are reportedly raising ~$1B to buy the company back, possibly toward a Hong Kong listing. *(Jun 2026)*
- **Google loses two more Gemini researchers to Anthropic** — Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel departed, the second pair to leave in a single week, following Noam Shazeer (original Transformer co-author, to OpenAI) and AlphaFold's Nobel laureate John Jumper (to Anthropic). *(Jun 2026)*

## 🚀 Model / Product Launches

- **OpenAI previews the GPT-5.6 family: Sol, Terra, Luna** — New naming scheme (flagship / everyday workhorse / fast-and-cheap), plus a new max-reasoning mode and an "ultra" mode that spins up subagents. Sol prices at $5 in / $30 out per million tokens. OpenAI says it spent over 700,000 A100-equivalent GPU hours on automated red-teaming for universal jailbreaks, building safeguards into model behaviour rather than bolting on a filter. *(Jun 2026)*
- **OpenAI + Broadcom unveil Jalapeño** — OpenAI's first in-house inference chip, taped out from design to manufacturing in a claimed nine months; a purpose-built LLM inference accelerator rather than a repurposed training part, with OpenAI's own models reportedly assisting the design. *(Jun 2026)*
- **Salesforce acquires Fin (~$3.6B)** — Bolts fast-deploy support agents onto Agentforce; the quieter prize is Apex, Fin's own support-tuned model, which Salesforce claims beats frontier OpenAI and Anthropic models on support resolution. *(Jun 2026)*

## 💬 Opinion & Analysis

- ChatGPT's share of the global AI-assistant market slipped below 50% for the first time (46.4%, per Sensor Tower), a milestone that got overshadowed by the export-control drama but marks a real shift in the competitive landscape.
- For two straight weeks, the strongest models of the cycle got held back not by what they could do, but by who was allowed to use them — arguably one of the most consequential shifts in how frontier AI gets governed so far this year.
- These days the hard part increasingly isn't building the technology, it's getting cleared to ship it — a dynamic that will keep intensifying as the US–China AI competition continues to escalate.
